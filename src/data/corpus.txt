# Chapter 1: Arjuna Visada Yoga (Arjuna's Despair)
1.1 [Dhritarashtra] Dhritarashtra said: Sanjaya, on the sacred field of Kurukshetra, the field of dharma, my sons and the sons of Pandu gathered, eager to fight. What did they do?
1.2 [Sanjaya] Sanjaya said: Seeing the Pandava army drawn up in battle formation, King Duryodhana then went up to his teacher, Drona, and spoke these words.
1.3 [Sanjaya] "Teacher, look at this vast army of the sons of Pandu, arranged for battle by the son of Drupada, your own gifted student."
1.4 [Sanjaya] "Here are heroes and mighty archers, equal in battle to Bhima and Arjuna: Yuyudhana, Virata, and Drupada, the great chariot-warrior;"
1.5 [Sanjaya] "Dhrishtaketu, Chekitana and the valiant king of Kashi; Purujit, Kuntibhoja, and Shaibya, the finest among men;"
1.6 [Sanjaya] "the bold Yudhamanyu and the valiant Uttamaujas, the son of Subhadra, and the sons of Draupadi: every one of them a great chariot-warrior."
1.7 [Sanjaya] "Now, best of the twice-born, hear about the most distinguished on our side, the leaders of my army. I name them for your information."
1.8 [Sanjaya] "There is you yourself, Bhishma, Karna, and Kripa, ever victorious in battle; Ashvatthama, Vikarna, and also the son of Somadatta."
1.9 [Sanjaya] "And there are many other heroes ready to give their lives for me, armed with many kinds of weapons, all of them skilled in war."
1.10 [Sanjaya] "Our strength, guarded by Bhishma, is beyond measure, while their strength, guarded by Bhima, is limited."
1.11 [Sanjaya] "So all of you, holding your assigned positions at every point of entry, must above all protect Bhishma."
1.12 [Sanjaya] Then, to lift Duryodhana's spirits, Bhishma, the mighty elder of the Kurus and their grandsire, let out a lion's roar and blew his conch loudly.
1.13 [Sanjaya] At once conches and kettledrums, tabors, drums and horns all burst into sound, and the noise became a tremendous uproar.
1.14 [Sanjaya] Then Krishna and Arjuna, standing in their great chariot yoked to white horses, blew their divine conches.
1.15 [Sanjaya] Krishna blew Panchajanya, Arjuna blew Devadatta, and Bhima, doer of fearsome deeds, blew the great conch Paundra.
1.16 [Sanjaya] King Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, blew Anantavijaya, while Nakula and Sahadeva blew Sughosha and Manipushpaka.
1.17 [Sanjaya] The king of Kashi, a superb archer, Shikhandi the great chariot-warrior, Dhrishtadyumna, Virata, and the unconquered Satyaki;
1.18 [Sanjaya] O ruler of the earth, Drupada and Draupadi's sons, along with Subhadra's mighty-armed son, all sounded their conches, one after another, from every side.
1.19 [Sanjaya] That tremendous sound, echoing across sky and earth, tore through the hearts of Dhritarashtra's sons.
1.20 [Sanjaya] Then Arjuna, whose banner bore the monkey emblem, saw Dhritarashtra's sons standing in their ranks. As weapons were about to clash, he raised his bow…
1.21 [Arjuna] …and, O king, spoke these words to Krishna. Arjuna said: Krishna, place my chariot between the two armies,
1.22 [Arjuna] so that I may look at those standing here eager for battle, and see whom I must fight in this coming war,
1.23 [Arjuna] and see those who have gathered here to fight, wanting to please the wicked-minded son of Dhritarashtra in this war.
1.24 [Sanjaya] Sanjaya said: O descendant of Bharata, when Arjuna had spoken this way, Krishna placed that finest of chariots between the two armies,
1.25 [Sanjaya] facing Bhishma, Drona and all the rulers of the earth, and said: "Arjuna, look at these Kurus gathered here."
1.26 [Sanjaya] There Arjuna saw standing fathers and grandfathers, teachers, uncles, brothers, sons, grandsons and friends,
1.27 [Sanjaya] as well as fathers-in-law and well-wishers in both armies. Seeing all these kinsmen arrayed there, Arjuna, son of Kunti,…
1.28 [Arjuna] …was overcome with deep compassion and spoke in sorrow. Arjuna said: Krishna, seeing my own people standing here, eager to fight,
1.29 [Arjuna] my limbs give way and my mouth is going dry. My body trembles and my hair stands on end.
1.30 [Arjuna] My bow Gandiva is slipping from my hand and my skin is burning. I cannot stand steady, and my mind seems to be spinning.
1.31 [Arjuna] Krishna, I see ill omens, and I see no good in killing my own people in battle.
1.32 [Arjuna] Krishna, I do not want victory, nor a kingdom, nor pleasures. What use to us is a kingdom, Govinda, or enjoyments, or even life itself?
1.33 [Arjuna] The very people for whose sake we wanted kingdom, enjoyment and happiness stand here in battle, ready to give up their lives and wealth:
1.34 [Arjuna] Here stand gurus, fathers and sons, and grandfathers too; mother's brothers, wives' fathers, grandchildren, wives' brothers, and kinsmen by marriage.
1.35 [Arjuna] Krishna, I do not wish to kill them, even if they kill me, not even for rule over the three worlds, let alone for this earth.
1.36 [Arjuna] What joy could we have from killing the sons of Dhritarashtra, Krishna? Though they are aggressors, only sin would cling to us if we killed them.
1.37 [Arjuna] So it is not right for us to kill the sons of Dhritarashtra, our own kin. How could we ever be happy after killing our own people, Krishna?
1.38 [Arjuna] Even if they, their minds overpowered by greed, do not see the wrong in destroying a family or the sin in betraying friends,
1.39 [Arjuna] why should we, who clearly see the harm in destroying a family, not know enough to turn away from this sin, Krishna?
1.40 [Arjuna] When a family is destroyed, its age-old traditions of dharma perish. And when dharma is lost, lawlessness overtakes the whole family.
1.41 [Arjuna] When lawlessness prevails, Krishna, the women of the family become corrupted; and when the women are corrupted, the social order becomes confused.
1.42 [Arjuna] This confusion leads the family and those who destroyed it to hell. Their ancestors fall too, deprived of the offerings of food and water.
1.43 [Arjuna] Through these wrongs of the family-destroyers, which create confusion in the social order, the lasting traditions of community and family are swept away.
1.44 [Arjuna] Krishna, we have heard that people whose family traditions are destroyed are sure to dwell in hell.
1.45 [Arjuna] Alas, how strange that we are set on committing a great sin, ready to kill our own people out of greed for the pleasures of a kingdom!
1.46 [Arjuna] It would be better for me if the sons of Dhritarashtra, weapons in hand, killed me in battle while I stood unarmed and did not resist.
1.47 [Sanjaya] Sanjaya said: Having spoken this way on the battlefield, Arjuna cast aside his bow and arrows and sank down on the seat of his chariot, his mind overwhelmed with grief.

# Chapter 2: Sankhya Yoga (Knowledge and the Undying Self)
2.1 [Sanjaya] Sanjaya said: Arjuna was overcome with pity, his eyes troubled and full of tears, sinking into despair. Krishna then spoke these words to him.
2.2 [Krishna] Krishna said: Arjuna, where has this weakness come from at such a critical moment? It is unworthy of a noble person; it leads neither to heaven nor to honour.
2.3 [Krishna] Arjuna, do not give in to this unmanliness; it does not suit you. Shake off this petty faint-heartedness and stand up, you who scorch your foes.
2.4 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: Krishna, how can I fight Bhishma and Drona with arrows in battle? They deserve my reverence.
2.5 [Arjuna] It would be better to live in this world even by begging than to kill these noble elders. If I kill my elders, however much they seek gain, every pleasure I enjoy here will be stained with blood.
2.6 [Arjuna] We do not even know which is better for us: that we conquer them, or that they conquer us. The sons of Dhritarashtra stand before us, and if we killed them we would not even wish to live.
2.7 [Arjuna] My natural strength is undone by weakness and pity, and I am confused about what my dharma is. I ask you: tell me clearly what is truly good for me. I am your disciple; I have come to you for refuge. Guide me.
2.8 [Arjuna] I see nothing that could take away this grief that is drying up my senses, not even gaining an unrivalled, prosperous kingdom on earth, or even lordship over the gods.
2.9 [Sanjaya] Sanjaya said: Having said this to Krishna, Arjuna, the conqueror of foes, declared, "I will not fight," and fell silent.
2.10 [Sanjaya] Then, O Dhritarashtra, between the two armies, Krishna, as if smiling, spoke these words to the despairing Arjuna.
2.11 [Krishna] Krishna said: Your sorrow is for people who do not call for sorrow, while your words carry the sound of learning. Those who truly understand mourn no one, whether their breath has gone or remains.
2.12 [Krishna] At no point did I not exist, and the same holds for you and for these rulers of men. Nor will a day come, from here onward, when any of us stops being.
2.13 [Krishna] Just as the embodied Self passes through childhood, youth and old age in this body, so too it passes into another body. A steady person is not confused by this.
2.14 [Krishna] Arjuna, when the senses meet their objects, we feel cold and warmth, comfort and distress. These touches arrive and depart; none of them endures. Learn to endure them, son of Kunti.
2.15 [Krishna] Arjuna, the steady person who is not shaken by these, who stays the same in pain and pleasure, is fit for immortality.
2.16 [Krishna] The unreal never comes into being; the real never ceases to be. Those who see the truth have understood the final nature of both.
2.17 [Krishna] Know that what pervades this whole universe is indestructible. No one can bring about the destruction of that which is imperishable.
2.18 [Krishna] These bodies are said to come to an end, but the one who dwells in them is eternal, indestructible and beyond measure. So fight, Arjuna.
2.19 [Krishna] One who thinks the Self is a killer, and one who thinks it is killed, both fail to understand. It neither kills nor is killed.
2.20 [Krishna] Birth and death never happen to the Self at any time. Having once been, it does not later stop being. It is unborn, constant, everlasting and primeval; when the body is struck down, the Self remains untouched.
2.21 [Krishna] Arjuna, when a person knows the Self to be indestructible, eternal, unborn and unchanging, how can that person kill anyone, or cause anyone to be killed?
2.22 [Krishna] Just as a person throws away worn-out clothes and puts on new ones, so the embodied Self leaves worn-out bodies and enters new ones.
2.23 [Krishna] Weapons cannot cut the Self, fire cannot burn it, water cannot wet it, and wind cannot dry it.
2.24 [Krishna] It cannot be cut, burned, wetted or dried. It is eternal, present everywhere, stable, unmoving and timeless.
2.25 [Krishna] It is said to be unmanifest, beyond thought and beyond change. So, knowing it to be like this, you should not grieve.
2.26 [Krishna] And even if you think of the Self as constantly being born and constantly dying, even then, mighty Arjuna, you should not grieve.
2.27 [Krishna] Death is certain for whatever is born, and birth is certain for whatever dies. So you should not grieve over what cannot be avoided.
2.28 [Krishna] Beings are unmanifest in their beginning, manifest in their middle, and unmanifest again at their end, Arjuna. What is there to lament in this?
2.29 [Krishna] One person looks upon the Self in amazement; another describes it with amazement; yet another listens to talk of it, amazed. Even having heard all this, nobody really comprehends it.
2.30 [Krishna] Arjuna, the Self that dwells in every body is eternal and can never be slain. So you should not grieve for any being.
2.31 [Krishna] Considering your own dharma as well, you should not waver. For a warrior, there is nothing better than a battle fought for what is right.
2.32 [Krishna] Arjuna, fortunate are the warriors who come upon such a battle unsought, like an open door to heaven.
2.33 [Krishna] But if you do not fight this righteous battle, you will abandon your dharma and your honour, and you will incur sin.
2.34 [Krishna] People will speak of your disgrace for ages to come. And for someone who has been honoured, disgrace is worse than death.
2.35 [Krishna] The great warriors will think you withdrew from battle out of fear, and those who held you in high regard will think little of you.
2.36 [Krishna] Your enemies will say many things that should never be said, mocking your strength. What could be more painful than that?
2.37 [Krishna] If you are killed, you will reach heaven; if you win, you will enjoy the earth. So rise, son of Kunti, resolved to fight.
2.38 [Krishna] Hold comfort and distress, profit and loss, winning and losing in equal regard, and with that balance prepare yourself to fight. Fighting like this, you will carry no wrong.
2.39 [Krishna] What I have told you so far is the understanding of Sankhya. Now hear it as it applies to yoga. Arjuna, joined with this understanding, you will break free from the bondage of action.
2.40 [Krishna] On this path no effort is wasted and no harm comes from it. Even a little of this practice protects one from great fear.
2.41 [Krishna] Arjuna, here the resolute mind is single and focused. But the thoughts of those who lack resolve are endless and branch out in many directions.
2.42 [Krishna] Arjuna, the unwise speak flowery words. Delighting in the letter of the Vedas, they claim there is nothing beyond it.
2.43 [Krishna] Full of desire and intent on heaven, they speak of elaborate rituals that promise rebirth as the fruit of action and aim at pleasure and power.
2.44 [Krishna] Those attached to pleasure and power, whose minds are carried away by such talk, do not develop the resolute understanding that settles in deep concentration.
2.45 [Krishna] The Vedas deal with the three gunas. Arjuna, rise above the three gunas. Be free of the pairs of opposites, always rooted in sattva, unconcerned with getting and keeping, and centred in the Self.
2.46 [Krishna] As much use as there is for a small well when water floods everywhere, that much use is there in all the Vedas for one who knows the truth.
2.47 [Krishna] You have a right to your actions alone, never to their results. Don't let results be your motive, and don't cling to doing nothing.
2.48 [Krishna] Arjuna, do your work steady in yoga, letting go of attachment and staying even-minded in success and failure. This evenness of mind is called yoga.
2.49 [Krishna] Action done for its own sake is far inferior to action guided by the yoga of understanding, Arjuna. Take refuge in that understanding. Pitiable are those whose motive is the result.
2.50 [Krishna] One joined with this understanding leaves behind both good and bad deeds even in this life. So devote yourself to yoga. Yoga is skill in action.
2.51 [Krishna] The wise, joined with this understanding, give up the fruits born of action. Freed from the bondage of birth, they reach the state beyond all suffering.
2.52 [Krishna] When your understanding crosses beyond the thicket of delusion, you will become indifferent to all you have heard and all that is yet to be heard.
2.53 [Krishna] When your mind, now confused by conflicting scriptural teachings, stands firm and unwavering in deep concentration, then you will attain yoga.
2.54 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: Krishna, how would you describe a person of steady wisdom, settled in deep concentration? How does such a person speak, how do they sit, and how do they move?
2.55 [Krishna] Krishna said: Arjuna, when a person lets go of all the desires that arise in the mind and is content in the Self, by the Self alone, they are said to be of steady wisdom.
2.56 [Krishna] Someone untroubled when sorrows come, who no longer hungers for pleasures, from whom craving, dread and rage have fallen away: such a silent one is called a sage whose understanding holds firm.
2.57 [Krishna] One who is without attachment anywhere, who neither rejoices at good fortune nor resents bad fortune when it comes, has wisdom that is firmly established.
2.58 [Krishna] When a person can fully draw the senses back from their objects, as a tortoise draws in its limbs, their wisdom is firmly established.
2.59 [Krishna] When an embodied person stops feeding the senses, the sense objects fall away, but the craving for them remains. Once the Supreme is seen, even that craving falls away.
2.60 [Krishna] Son of Kunti, the turbulent senses forcibly carry away the mind even of a wise person who is striving to control them.
2.61 [Krishna] Restraining all the senses, one should sit disciplined, with Me as the highest goal. One whose senses are under control has wisdom that is firmly established.
2.62 [Krishna] When a person dwells on sense objects, attachment to them arises. From attachment comes desire, and from desire comes anger.
2.63 [Krishna] Anger leads to delusion, and delusion to confused memory. When memory is confused, understanding is destroyed, and when understanding is destroyed, a person is ruined.
2.64 [Krishna] But a self-controlled person who moves among sense objects with senses free from attraction and aversion, and held under their own command, attains inner clarity and peace.
2.65 [Krishna] In that clarity, all sorrows come to an end. For the one whose mind is clear, understanding soon becomes firmly steady.
2.66 [Krishna] For one who is undisciplined there is no understanding, and no meditation either. Without meditation there is no peace, and without peace, how can there be happiness?
2.67 [Krishna] When the mind follows the wandering senses, it carries away one's understanding, just as the wind carries away a boat on the water.
2.68 [Krishna] So, mighty Arjuna, one whose senses are fully held back from their objects has wisdom that is firmly established.
2.69 [Krishna] What is night for all beings is when the self-controlled person is awake; and what all beings are awake to is night for the sage who truly sees.
2.70 [Krishna] Just as rivers flow into the ocean, which is always being filled yet stays steady within its bounds, so the one into whom all desires flow in the same way attains peace, not the one who chases desires.
2.71 [Krishna] One who gives up all desires and moves through life without craving, without the sense of 'mine' and without ego, attains peace.
2.72 [Krishna] Arjuna, this is the state of being rooted in Brahman. Having reached it, one is no longer deluded. Established in it even at the time of death, one attains the freedom of Brahman.

# Chapter 3: Karma Yoga (The Path of Action)
3.1 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: Krishna, if you hold that understanding is superior to action, then why do you push me into this terrible deed?
3.2 [Arjuna] Your words seem mixed and contradictory, and they confuse my mind. Tell me, decisively, the one path by which I can reach what is truly good.
3.3 [Krishna] Krishna said: Sinless Arjuna, as I explained earlier, there are two ways of commitment in this world: the path of knowledge for those given to reflection (Sankhya), and the path of action for the yogis.
3.4 [Krishna] A person does not reach freedom from action simply by not starting any work, nor does anyone attain perfection by renunciation alone.
3.5 [Krishna] No one can stay even for a moment without doing something. Everyone is driven, helplessly, to act by the gunas that arise from nature (prakriti).
3.6 [Krishna] Whoever holds back the organs of action but sits dwelling in the mind on the objects of the senses is deceiving themselves, and is called a hypocrite.
3.7 [Krishna] But one who reins in the senses with the mind, Arjuna, and without attachment sets the organs of action to the yoga of action, stands far above.
3.8 [Krishna] Do the work that is yours to do, for action is better than inaction. Without acting, you could not even keep your body going.
3.9 [Krishna] Apart from action done as yajna, an offering, this world is bound by its actions. So, son of Kunti, act for that purpose, free from attachment.
3.10 [Krishna] In the beginning, the Creator brought forth beings together with yajna and said: "Through this you will grow and multiply; let this be the source that grants all you desire."
3.11 [Krishna] "Nourish the gods with this, and let the gods nourish you. By supporting one another, you will reach the highest good."
3.12 [Krishna] "Pleased by yajna, the gods will give you the enjoyments you wish for." Whoever enjoys their gifts without offering anything back to them is nothing but a thief.
3.13 [Krishna] Good people who eat what remains after the offering are freed from all faults. But the wicked who cook only for their own sake are eating sin.
3.14 [Krishna] Beings come from food, food comes from rain, rain comes from yajna, and yajna is born of action.
3.15 [Krishna] Know that action has its source in the Veda (brahma), and the Veda springs from the Imperishable. So the all-pervading Brahman is always present in yajna.
3.16 [Krishna] Whoever does not help turn this wheel that has been set in motion lives a life of wrongdoing, finding pleasure only in the senses. Such a person lives in vain, Arjuna.
3.17 [Krishna] But for a person whose delight is in the Self alone, who is satisfied in the Self and content within the Self, nothing remains that must be done.
3.18 [Krishna] Such a person has nothing to gain here by acting, and nothing by not acting. Nor do they depend on any being for any purpose.
3.19 [Krishna] So, without attachment, keep doing the work that ought to be done. By acting without attachment, a person reaches the highest.
3.20 [Krishna] Janaka and others reached perfection through action itself. You too should act, keeping in view the welfare and upholding of the world.
3.21 [Krishna] Whatever an outstanding person does, others do the same. Whatever standard such a person sets, the world follows it.
3.22 [Krishna] Arjuna, there is nothing in all three worlds that I am bound to do, nothing I lack and need to gain. Yet I remain engaged in action.
3.23 [Krishna] For if I ever stopped acting tirelessly, people everywhere would follow my path, Arjuna.
3.24 [Krishna] If I did not act, these worlds would fall into ruin. I would be the cause of confusion and disorder, and would bring these people to harm.
3.25 [Krishna] Just as the unwise act out of attachment to their work, Arjuna, the wise should act without attachment, wanting to hold the world together.
3.26 [Krishna] A person of knowledge should not stir doubt in the minds of those who cling to their work without understanding. Working in a balanced, disciplined way, the wise one should help them find joy in every duty.
3.27 [Krishna] All actions are carried out entirely by the gunas of nature. But one whose mind is clouded by ego thinks, "I am the doer."
3.28 [Krishna] But one who knows the truth about how the gunas and their actions are distinct, mighty Arjuna, sees that it is only gunas acting upon gunas, and does not become attached.
3.29 [Krishna] Those bewildered by the gunas of nature cling to the workings of the gunas. One who sees the whole should not disturb these slow people of partial understanding.
3.30 [Krishna] Hand over all your actions to me, with your mind resting on the Self. Free from craving and from the sense of "mine", fight, with your inner fever gone.
3.31 [Krishna] People who steadily follow this teaching of mine, with faith and without finding fault, are also freed from the bonds of action.
3.32 [Krishna] But those who find fault with it and do not follow my teaching, know them to be confused about all knowledge, lost and lacking discernment.
3.33 [Krishna] Even a wise person acts in keeping with their own nature. All beings follow their nature; what can forced suppression achieve?
3.34 [Krishna] Attraction and aversion sit waiting in each sense toward its objects. Do not come under their control, for they are the enemies along a person's path.
3.35 [Krishna] Better your own dharma, even if done imperfectly, than another's dharma done well. Better to die in your own dharma; another's dharma brings danger.
3.36 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: Then what drives a person to do wrong, Krishna, even against their own wishes, as if pushed by force?
3.37 [Krishna] Krishna said: It is desire, it is anger, born of the guna of rajas. Insatiable and deeply harmful, know this to be the enemy here.
3.38 [Krishna] As fire is veiled by smoke, a mirror by dust, and an unborn child by the womb, so this is veiled by desire.
3.39 [Krishna] Knowledge is covered, son of Kunti, by this constant enemy of the wise, which takes the form of desire, a fire that can never be filled.
3.40 [Krishna] This desire is said to have its seat in the senses, the mind and the intellect. Working through these, it clouds understanding and leads the embodied one astray.
3.41 [Krishna] So, Arjuna, first bring your senses under control, and then destroy this evil, which ruins both knowledge and realisation.
3.42 [Krishna] The senses are said to be subtle and high; higher than the senses is the mind; higher than the mind is the intellect; and beyond the intellect is the Self.
3.43 [Krishna] Knowing in this way what lies beyond the intellect, and steadying yourself through the Self, mighty Arjuna, destroy this enemy in the form of desire, so hard to overcome.

# Chapter 4: Jnana Karma Sanyasa Yoga (Knowledge, Action and Renunciation)
4.1 [Krishna] Krishna said: I taught this imperishable yoga to Vivasvan, the sun. Vivasvan passed it on to Manu, and Manu told it to Ikshvaku.
4.2 [Krishna] Handed down in this way from one to the next, it was known to the royal sages. But over a great span of time, Arjuna, this yoga was lost to the world.
4.3 [Krishna] That same ancient yoga I have explained to you today, because you are my devotee and my friend. It is indeed the deepest secret.
4.4 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: Your birth came late, while Vivasvan's came far earlier. So how can I make sense of your saying that you were the one who first taught this?
4.5 [Krishna] Krishna said: Many births of mine have come and gone, Arjuna, and many of yours too. I know them all, but you do not.
4.6 [Krishna] Though I am unborn and my being never perishes, and though I am Lord of all beings, I come into being by taking charge of my own nature, through my own power (maya).
4.7 [Krishna] Whenever dharma weakens and adharma rises, Arjuna, I bring myself forth.
4.8 [Krishna] To protect the good, to destroy those who do evil, and to set dharma firmly in place, I appear in age after age.
4.9 [Krishna] One who truly understands my divine birth and actions in this way is not born again on leaving the body, but comes to me, Arjuna.
4.10 [Krishna] Free from craving, fear and anger, absorbed in me and relying on me, many people, purified by the discipline of knowledge, have come to share my nature.
4.11 [Krishna] However people come to me, in that same way I respond to them. In every way, Arjuna, people follow my path.
4.12 [Krishna] Wanting their actions to succeed, people here worship the gods, for in the human world success that comes from action arrives quickly.
4.13 [Krishna] The fourfold social order was created by me according to the distribution of gunas and actions. Though I am its maker, know me as the unchanging one who does not act.
4.14 [Krishna] Actions do not stain me, and I have no longing for their results. Whoever knows me in this way is not bound by actions.
4.15 [Krishna] Knowing this, those of old who sought freedom also acted. So you too should act, just as the ancients did long before.
4.16 [Krishna] What is action, and what is inaction? Even the wise are confused about this. I will explain action to you, and knowing it you will be freed from what is harmful.
4.17 [Krishna] One has to understand action, one has to understand wrong action, and one has to understand inaction. The way of action is deep and hard to grasp.
4.18 [Krishna] Whoever can recognise stillness within activity and activity within stillness has real insight among human beings. That person is joined in yoga, even while carrying out every kind of work.
4.19 [Krishna] One whose every undertaking is free from desire and self-centred intention, whose actions have been burnt up in the fire of knowledge, is called learned by the wise.
4.20 [Krishna] Having let go of attachment to the results of action, always content and leaning on nothing, such a person does nothing at all, even while fully engaged in action.
4.21 [Krishna] Expecting nothing, with mind and self restrained, having given up all possessiveness, and doing only what the body needs, one incurs no fault.
4.22 [Krishna] Content with whatever comes on its own, beyond the pairs of opposites, free from envy, and even-minded in success and failure, such a person is not bound even when acting.
4.23 [Krishna] For one who is free from attachment and liberated, whose mind is grounded in knowledge, and who acts as an offering, all action melts away entirely.
4.24 [Krishna] The act of offering is Brahman, the offering is Brahman, offered by Brahman into the fire that is Brahman. One who sees Brahman in every action reaches Brahman alone.
4.25 [Krishna] Some yogis carry out yajna as worship of the gods. Others offer yajna itself, as an offering, into the fire of Brahman.
4.26 [Krishna] Some offer hearing and the other senses into the fires of restraint. Others offer sound and the other objects of sense into the fires of the senses.
4.27 [Krishna] Others offer all the workings of the senses and of the life-breath into the fire of the yoga of self-control, lit by knowledge.
4.28 [Krishna] Some offer their wealth, some their austerity, some their practice of yoga. Others, striving earnestly under strict vows, offer study of scripture and knowledge.
4.29 [Krishna] Others, devoted to the control of breath, offer the in-breath into the out-breath and the out-breath into the in-breath, holding back the movement of both.
4.30 [Krishna] Still others, limiting their food, offer the life-breaths into the life-breaths. All these understand yajna, and through yajna their impurities are worn away.
4.31 [Krishna] Those who eat the nectar that remains from yajna reach the eternal Brahman. Even this world is not for one who makes no offering; what then of any other, Arjuna?
4.32 [Krishna] Many kinds of yajna like these are laid out in the words of the Veda. Know that they all arise from action; knowing this, you will be set free.
4.33 [Krishna] The yajna of knowledge is better than a yajna of material things, Arjuna. All action, in its entirety, finds its completion in knowledge.
4.34 [Krishna] Learn this by humbly bowing, by sincere questioning, and by service. Those who know and have seen the truth will teach you that knowledge.
4.35 [Krishna] When you have gained that knowledge, son of Pandu, confusion like this will not seize you again. By its light you will find every single creature within yourself, and then within me.
4.36 [Krishna] Even if you were the worst of all wrongdoers, you would cross over every wrong on the raft of knowledge alone.
4.37 [Krishna] As a blazing fire reduces firewood to ash, Arjuna, so the fire of knowledge reduces all actions to ash.
4.38 [Krishna] Nothing in this world purifies like knowledge. One who has been perfected through yoga finds it within, in time.
4.39 [Krishna] One who has faith, who is devoted to it and has mastered the senses, gains knowledge. Having gained it, such a person soon reaches the highest peace.
4.40 [Krishna] One who is ignorant, without faith and full of doubt is ruined. For the doubting mind there is neither this world, nor the next, nor any happiness.
4.41 [Krishna] Actions do not bind one who has given up action through yoga, whose doubts have been cut away by knowledge, and who is centred in the Self, Arjuna.
4.42 [Krishna] So with the sword of knowledge, cut away this doubt in your heart, born of ignorance. Take your stand in yoga, and rise, Arjuna!

# Chapter 5: Karma Sanyasa Yoga (True Renunciation)
5.1 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: Krishna, you praise the renunciation of action, and then again the yoga of action. Tell me clearly which one of the two is better.
5.2 [Krishna] Krishna said: Giving up action and practising the yoga of action each bring a person to the supreme good. Between them, though, acting through yoga surpasses abandoning action.
5.3 [Krishna] Know that one who neither hates nor craves is a true renunciate at all times. Free from the pairs of opposites, mighty Arjuna, such a person is easily released from bondage.
5.4 [Krishna] It is the naive, not the wise, who speak of Sankhya and yoga as different. One who is truly established in even one of them gains the fruit of both.
5.5 [Krishna] The state reached by followers of Sankhya is reached by the yogis as well. One who sees Sankhya and yoga as one truly sees.
5.6 [Krishna] But renunciation, mighty Arjuna, is hard to reach without yoga. The sage who is steady in yoga reaches Brahman before long.
5.7 [Krishna] One who is steady in yoga, pure within, master of the self and the senses, whose self has become the self of all beings, is not tainted even while acting.
5.8 [Krishna] One who is steady in yoga and knows the truth thinks, "I am not doing anything at all," even while seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, walking, sleeping and breathing…
5.9 [Krishna] …while speaking, letting go, taking hold, and opening and closing the eyes, holding firmly that it is only the senses moving among their objects.
5.10 [Krishna] One who acts by placing all actions in Brahman and letting go of attachment is not touched by wrongdoing, just as a lotus leaf is not wetted by water.
5.11 [Krishna] Letting go of attachment, yogis act with the body, the mind, the intellect, and even with the senses alone, to purify themselves.
5.12 [Krishna] One who is steady in yoga, giving up the fruit of action, gains lasting peace. One who is not, driven by desire and attached to the fruit, is bound.
5.13 [Krishna] Having renounced all actions in the mind, the self-controlled embodied soul rests happily in the city of nine gates, the body, neither acting nor causing anything to be done.
5.14 [Krishna] The Lord does not create a sense of doership in people, nor their actions, nor the link between action and its result. It is one's own nature that is at work.
5.15 [Krishna] The all-pervading one takes on neither anyone's wrongdoing nor anyone's good deeds. Knowledge is covered by ignorance, and that is why beings are deluded.
5.16 [Krishna] But in those whose ignorance has been destroyed by knowledge of the Self, that knowledge, like the sun, lights up the Supreme.
5.17 [Krishna] With their intellect fixed on That, their self one with That, grounded in That and holding That as their goal, their impurities washed away by knowledge, they reach the state from which there is no return.
5.18 [Krishna] The wise look with an equal eye on a learned and humble brahmin, a cow, an elephant, a dog, and an outcaste who eats dog meat.
5.19 [Krishna] Those whose minds rest in evenness have overcome the cycle of birth here and now. Brahman is flawless and the same in all, so they are established in Brahman.
5.20 [Krishna] One who knows Brahman and abides in Brahman, steady in mind and free from delusion, neither rejoices at gaining what is pleasant nor is shaken by meeting what is unpleasant.
5.21 [Krishna] One who is not attached to outer contacts finds the joy that lies within the Self. United with Brahman through yoga, such a person enjoys a happiness that never ends.
5.22 [Krishna] Pleasures born of contact with the senses are only sources of sorrow, son of Kunti, for they begin and they end. The wise take no delight in them.
5.23 [Krishna] One who can withstand, here in this life, before leaving the body, the surge that arises from desire and anger is steady in yoga, and is a truly happy person.
5.24 [Krishna] One whose happiness is within, whose delight is within, and whose light is within, that yogi, having become one with Brahman, attains the peace of Brahman (brahma-nirvana).
5.25 [Krishna] Sages whose impurities are gone, whose doubts have been cut away, who are self-controlled and devoted to the good of all beings, attain the peace of Brahman.
5.26 [Krishna] For seekers free from desire and anger, whose minds are controlled and who know the Self, the peace of Brahman is near on every side.
5.27 [Krishna] Shutting out external contacts, fixing the gaze between the eyebrows, and evening out the in-breath and out-breath that move within the nostrils…
5.28 [Krishna] …with senses, mind and intellect controlled, the sage whose goal is liberation, free of desire, fear and anger, is truly free forever.
5.29 [Krishna] When someone recognises me as the enjoyer of every offering and every austerity, the mighty ruler over all worlds, and a well-wisher to every creature, that person comes to peace.

# Chapter 6: Dhyana Yoga (Meditation and Self-Mastery)
6.1 [Krishna] Krishna said: Whoever does the work that ought to be done without leaning on its fruits is both a renunciant and a yogi, not the one who has merely given up the sacred fire or stopped all activity.
6.2 [Krishna] What people call renunciation, know that to be yoga, son of Pandu. No one becomes a yogi without first letting go of self-willed plans and intentions.
6.3 [Krishna] For the sage still trying to rise into yoga, work is named as the way forward. Once that same sage has reached the height of yoga, quietude becomes the way.
6.4 [Krishna] When a person no longer clings to the objects of the senses or to actions, and has let go of all self-willed intentions, then he is said to be established in yoga.
6.5 [Krishna] Lift yourself up by your own effort; do not let yourself sink down. For you alone are your own friend, and you alone are your own enemy.
6.6 [Krishna] For one who has mastered himself by himself, the self is a friend. But for one who has not mastered himself, his own self acts against him like an enemy.
6.7 [Krishna] One who has mastered himself and is deeply at peace remains centred in the highest Self, through cold and heat, pleasure and pain, honour and insult alike.
6.8 [Krishna] The yogi who is content with knowledge and its direct realisation, unshakeable, with senses mastered, who sees a lump of earth, a stone and gold as the same, is called truly steadfast.
6.9 [Krishna] Higher still is the one who looks with an even mind on well-wishers, friends and enemies, on the indifferent and the neutral, on the hateful and on kin, on the good and on the wicked.
6.10 [Krishna] A yogi should keep his mind constantly absorbed in the Self, staying in a secluded place, alone, with mind and body restrained, free of cravings and possessions.
6.11 [Krishna] In a clean place he should set up a firm seat for himself, neither too high nor too low, covered with kusha grass, then a deerskin, then a cloth.
6.12 [Krishna] Sitting there, making the mind one-pointed and holding thought and the senses in check, he should practise yoga to purify himself.
6.13 [Krishna] Holding body, head and neck straight and still, steady, he should rest his gaze at the tip of his nose without looking around in any direction.
6.14 [Krishna] Calm and fearless, firm in the vow of celibacy, mind restrained and thoughts fixed on me, he should sit in yoga, holding me as his highest goal.
6.15 [Krishna] Practising in this way, always keeping himself absorbed, the yogi of disciplined mind reaches the peace that rests in me, whose summit is nirvana.
6.16 [Krishna] Arjuna, yoga does not come to someone who overeats or to someone who goes entirely without food, nor to one given to oversleeping, nor to one who keeps going without rest.
6.17 [Krishna] For one who is measured in eating and recreation, measured in effort at work, and measured in sleep and waking, yoga becomes the destroyer of sorrow.
6.18 [Krishna] When the well-governed mind rests in the Self alone, free of longing for any desire, then a person is said to be steadfast in yoga.
6.19 [Krishna] "Like a lamp in a windless place that does not flicker": that is the image used for the yogi whose mind is controlled and who practises union with the Self.
6.20 [Krishna] When the mind, held still through the practice of yoga, comes to rest, and when, seeing the Self by the Self, one is content in the Self…
6.21 [Krishna] …when one knows that boundless joy which is grasped by the intellect and lies beyond the senses, and, standing in it, never wavers from the truth…
6.22 [Krishna] …having gained which one thinks no other gain greater, and established in which one is not shaken even by heavy sorrow…
6.23 [Krishna] …know that to be yoga: the breaking of all contact with sorrow. This yoga should be practised with determination and an undiscouraged mind.
6.24 [Krishna] Giving up completely all desires born of mental imagining, and reining in the whole crowd of senses from every side with the mind itself…
6.25 [Krishna] …one should little by little become still, through an intellect held firm by resolve. Settling the mind in the Self, one should think of nothing else at all.
6.26 [Krishna] Each time the fickle, wavering mind slips away, toward whatever it drifts, one should rein it in from there and lead it back to rest under the Self's guidance.
6.27 [Krishna] The highest joy comes to this yogi whose mind is completely at peace, whose restless passion has quieted, who is free of stain and has become one with Brahman.
6.28 [Krishna] Practising in this way, always keeping himself absorbed, the yogi freed from impurity easily attains the boundless joy of touching Brahman.
6.29 [Krishna] A person whose inner being is united through yoga perceives the Self dwelling in every creature and every creature resting in the Self, and looks upon all with an even eye.
6.30 [Krishna] When a person finds me in all places and finds all things within me, I never drop out of that person's sight, and that person never drops out of mine.
6.31 [Krishna] The yogi who, grounded in oneness, worships me as dwelling in all beings lives in me, however he may be living.
6.32 [Krishna] Arjuna, one who, taking himself as the measure, sees pleasure and pain everywhere with the same eye is considered the highest yogi.
6.33 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: Krishna, this yoga of evenness you have described, I cannot see how it can stay firm, because the mind is so restless.
6.34 [Arjuna] The mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, strong and stubborn. I think it is as hard to hold back as the wind.
6.35 [Krishna] Krishna said: No doubt, mighty Arjuna, the mind is restless and hard to control. But it can be brought under control, son of Kunti, through practice and detachment.
6.36 [Krishna] In my view, yoga is hard to reach for one who has not restrained himself. But one who has self-control and keeps striving can attain it by the right means.
6.37 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: Krishna, what becomes of a person who has faith but lacks self-discipline, whose mind slips away from yoga before reaching its fulfilment?
6.38 [Arjuna] Fallen from both, without support, lost on the path to Brahman, does he not perish, mighty Krishna, like a cloud torn apart?
6.39 [Arjuna] Krishna, please clear away this doubt of mine completely. There is no one but you who can remove it.
6.40 [Krishna] Krishna said: Arjuna, there is no ruin for him, neither in this world nor in the next. No one who does good, my friend, ever comes to a bad end.
6.41 [Krishna] Having reached the worlds of those who did good and lived there for countless years, one who fell from yoga is born again in the home of the pure and prosperous.
6.42 [Krishna] Or else he is born into a family of wise yogis. But a birth like this is very hard to come by in this world.
6.43 [Krishna] There he regains the understanding he had developed in his previous body, and from there he strives again, Arjuna, towards perfection.
6.44 [Krishna] By that very earlier practice he is carried forward, even without willing it. Even one who simply wants to know about yoga goes beyond the mere words of the Vedas.
6.45 [Krishna] But the yogi who strives with great effort, cleansed of faults and perfected over many births, then reaches the highest goal.
6.46 [Krishna] The yogi is greater than those who practise austerities, greater even than the learned, and greater than those devoted to ritual work. So be a yogi, Arjuna.
6.47 [Krishna] And of all yogis, the one who worships me with faith, his inner self gone into me, I consider the most deeply united with me.

# Chapter 7: Gyaan Vigyana Yoga (Knowing and Realising)
7.1 [Krishna] Krishna said: Partha, keep your mind fastened on me, practise yoga, and rely on me alone. Now listen, and learn how you can come to know me completely, free of all doubt.
7.2 [Krishna] I will explain to you completely this knowledge together with its direct realisation, knowing which nothing else here remains to be known.
7.3 [Krishna] Among thousands of people, hardly one strives for perfection. And even among those who strive and succeed, hardly one knows me as I truly am.
7.4 [Krishna] Earth, water, fire, air, space, mind, intellect and the sense of 'I': these are the eight divisions of my nature (prakriti).
7.5 [Krishna] This is my lower nature. But beyond it, mighty Arjuna, know my other, higher nature: the life-principle, by which this whole world is held up.
7.6 [Krishna] Understand that all beings arise from these two natures. I am the origin of this entire world, and also its dissolution.
7.7 [Krishna] There is nothing whatever higher than me, Arjuna. All of this is strung on me, like gems on a thread.
7.8 [Krishna] Arjuna, I am the flavour found in water and the brightness of moon and sun. I am Om within every Veda, sound within the sky, and the vigour within human beings.
7.9 [Krishna] I am the pure fragrance of the earth and the brilliance in fire. I am the life in all beings and the austerity in those who practise it.
7.10 [Krishna] Partha, recognise me as the timeless seed from which every creature springs. In those with understanding I am their understanding; in the splendid, I am their splendour.
7.11 [Krishna] I am the strength of the strong, free of craving and attachment. And in beings I am desire that does not go against dharma, Arjuna.
7.12 [Krishna] Whatever states of being there are, whether of sattva, rajas or tamas, know that they come from me alone. Yet I am not in them; they are in me.
7.13 [Krishna] Deluded by these three states made of the gunas, this whole world fails to recognise me, who am beyond them and imperishable.
7.14 [Krishna] This divine power of illusion (maya) of mine, made of the gunas, is very hard to get past. But those who take refuge in me alone cross over it.
7.15 [Krishna] Evildoers, the deluded and the lowest of people do not turn to me; their understanding is stolen by illusion and they adopt a demonic nature.
7.16 [Krishna] Four kinds of virtuous people worship me, Arjuna: the distressed, the seeker of knowledge, the one who seeks gain, and the one who knows.
7.17 [Krishna] Of these, the one who knows, always steadfast and devoted to one alone, stands out. For I am exceedingly dear to the one who knows, and he is dear to me.
7.18 [Krishna] All of these are noble, but the one who knows I regard as my very Self. For with his self united, he is established in me alone, the highest goal.
7.19 [Krishna] At the end of many births, the one who knows takes refuge in me, realising that Vasudeva is everything. Such a great soul is very rare.
7.20 [Krishna] Those whose understanding is carried off by one desire or another turn to other gods, following this or that observance, bound by their own nature.
7.21 [Krishna] Whatever form a devotee wishes to worship with faith, I make that very faith of his firm.
7.22 [Krishna] Endowed with that faith, he seeks to worship that form, and from it he gains what he desires; yet it is I alone who grant those things.
7.23 [Krishna] But the fruit gained by these people of small understanding is limited. Worshippers of the gods go to the gods; my devotees come to me.
7.24 [Krishna] The unwise think of me, the unmanifest, as having become manifest, not knowing my higher being, which is imperishable and unsurpassed.
7.25 [Krishna] Veiled by my own yoga-maya, I am not revealed to everyone. This deluded world does not recognise me as unborn and imperishable.
7.26 [Krishna] I know the beings of the past, of the present and of the future, Arjuna. But no one knows me.
7.27 [Krishna] From the moment of birth, Arjuna, all beings fall into confusion through the delusion of opposites that arises from liking and disliking.
7.28 [Krishna] Yet those whose good actions have brought their wrongdoing to a close are released from the confusion that pairs of opposites create. Steadfast in their vows, they turn to me in worship.
7.29 [Krishna] Those who take refuge in me and strive for freedom from old age and death come to know Brahman in full, the inner Self, and the whole of karma.
7.30 [Krishna] Those who know me along with the material realm, the divine realm and the realm of sacrifice, with their minds united, know me even at the hour of death.

# Chapter 8: Akshara Brahma Yoga (The Imperishable)
8.1 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: Krishna, what is that Brahman? What is the inner Self (adhyatma)? What is karma? What is called the material realm (adhibhuta), and what is said to be the divine realm (adhidaiva)?
8.2 [Arjuna] Who is the lord of sacrifice (adhiyajna) here in this body, and how, Krishna? And how are you to be known at the time of death by those who have mastered themselves?
8.3 [Krishna] Krishna said: Brahman is the supreme, the imperishable. Its own essential being is called the inner Self. The creative offering that brings beings into existence is called karma.
8.4 [Krishna] The material realm is perishable existence; the divine realm is the cosmic Person (purusha); and I myself, here in the body, am the lord of sacrifice, best of embodied beings.
8.5 [Krishna] Whoever, at the end, leaves the body remembering me alone, comes to my being. Of this there is no doubt.
8.6 [Krishna] Whatever state of being one remembers when finally leaving the body, son of Kunti, to that very state one goes, having always been shaped by it.
8.7 [Krishna] So at all times remember me, and fight. With mind and intellect offered to me, you will surely come to me.
8.8 [Krishna] Arjuna, with a mind trained by the yoga of practice and not straying elsewhere, constantly meditating, one reaches the supreme divine Person.
8.9 [Krishna] One should meditate on the all-knowing, the ancient, the ruler, subtler than the subtlest, the sustainer of all, whose form is beyond thought, radiant like the sun, beyond all darkness…
8.10 [Krishna] …at the time of departure, with an unwavering mind, filled with devotion and by the strength of yoga, drawing the life-breath fully between the eyebrows, one reaches that supreme divine Person.
8.11 [Krishna] That which knowers of the Vedas call the imperishable, which ascetics free of passion enter, and wishing for which people live a life of celibacy, that state I will explain to you briefly.
8.12 [Krishna] Closing all the gates of the senses, confining the mind in the heart, placing his life-breath in the head, and established in yogic concentration…
8.13 [Krishna] …uttering the single syllable Om, which is Brahman, and remembering me, whoever departs leaving the body reaches the highest goal.
8.14 [Krishna] Arjuna, for the yogi who remembers me constantly, every day, with a mind turned to nothing else, ever steadfast, I am easy to reach.
8.15 [Krishna] Having come to me, these great souls who have reached the highest perfection are not born again into this fleeting home of sorrow.
8.16 [Krishna] All worlds, up to the realm of Brahma, are subject to return, Arjuna. But for one who has come to me, son of Kunti, there is no rebirth.
8.17 [Krishna] Those who know that a day of Brahma lasts a thousand ages, and a night of Brahma likewise ends after a thousand ages, truly understand day and night.
8.18 [Krishna] When that day dawns, all manifest things emerge from the unmanifest. When night falls, they dissolve back into that same thing called the unmanifest.
8.19 [Krishna] This same multitude of beings, coming into being again and again, dissolves helplessly when night falls, Arjuna, and comes forth again when day dawns.
8.20 [Krishna] But beyond that unmanifest there is another, eternal unmanifest existence, which does not perish when all beings perish.
8.21 [Krishna] That unmanifest is called the imperishable, and it is said to be the highest goal. Those who reach it do not return. That is my supreme abode.
8.22 [Krishna] Partha, that highest Person holds all beings within himself and extends through this whole universe. Devotion turned toward nothing else is how he is attained.
8.23 [Krishna] Now, Arjuna, I will tell you the times at which yogis who depart do not return, and the times at which they do return.
8.24 [Krishna] Fire, brightness, the day, the waxing half of the moon, the six months when the sun travels north: knowers of Brahman who leave the body along this route reach Brahman.
8.25 [Krishna] Smoke, night, the dark fortnight, the six months of the sun's southward course: by this path the yogi reaches the light of the moon and then returns.
8.26 [Krishna] These two paths of the world, the bright and the dark, are regarded as eternal. By one a person goes and does not return; by the other he comes back again.
8.27 [Krishna] No yogi who knows these two paths is ever confused, Arjuna. So at all times, Arjuna, be steadfast in yoga.
8.28 [Krishna] Whatever merit is promised for study of the Vedas, for sacrifices, austerities and gifts, the yogi who knows this goes beyond it all and reaches the supreme, original abode.

# Chapter 9: Raja Vidya Yoga (The Royal Secret)
9.1 [Krishna] Krishna said: Because you do not find fault, I will now reveal to you this most hidden knowledge, together with the insight that comes from living it. Knowing it, you will be freed from all that is harmful.
9.2 [Krishna] This is the king of all knowledge and the king of all secrets, supremely purifying. It can be directly experienced, it is in harmony with dharma, it is joyful to practise, and it never fades.
9.3 [Krishna] Those who have no faith in this teaching, O Arjuna, do not reach me. They return to the path of death and repeated birth.
9.4 [Krishna] This whole universe is pervaded by me in my unmanifest form. All beings rest in me, but I do not rest in them.
9.5 [Krishna] And yet beings do not truly rest in me. See my divine power! My Self sustains all beings and brings them into existence, yet it does not dwell in them.
9.6 [Krishna] Just as the mighty wind, moving everywhere, always remains within space, understand that in the same way all beings remain within me.
9.7 [Krishna] At the end of a cosmic cycle, son of Kunti, all beings merge back into my own nature. At the start of the next cycle, I send them forth again.
9.8 [Krishna] Taking hold of my own nature, I send forth this whole multitude of beings again and again. They are helpless, governed by the force of nature.
9.9 [Krishna] These actions do not bind me, Arjuna, for I sit among them as one who is indifferent, unattached to all that is done.
9.10 [Krishna] Under my watch, nature gives birth to all that moves and all that does not. This is why, son of Kunti, the world keeps turning.
9.11 [Krishna] Deluded people scorn me when I take up a human form. They have no idea of my higher nature, in which I am supreme ruler over every being.
9.12 [Krishna] Their hopes are empty, their actions empty, their knowledge empty, and their minds confused. They take on the deluding nature of demons and fiends.
9.13 [Krishna] But the great-souled, son of Pritha, who take refuge in the divine nature, worship me with undivided minds, knowing me as the imperishable source of all beings.
9.14 [Krishna] Always singing my praises, striving with firm resolve, bowing to me with devotion, they worship me, ever united with me.
9.15 [Krishna] Others worship me through the offering of knowledge: some as the One, some as distinct from themselves, and others in many ways, as the one whose faces are everywhere.
9.16 [Krishna] The Vedic rite is me, and so is the offering; the oblation given to the forefathers is me, and so is the medicinal plant. I am the mantra, the ghee, the flame, and the oblation poured into it.
9.17 [Krishna] I am the father of this world, its mother, its sustainer and its grandsire. I am what is to be known, the purifier, the sacred syllable Om, and the Rig, Sama and Yajur Vedas.
9.18 [Krishna] I am the goal, the support, the master, the witness, the home, the refuge and the friend. I am the origin and the dissolution, the ground, the storehouse, and the seed that never perishes.
9.19 [Krishna] I give heat; I hold back the rain and I release it. I am immortality and I am death, Arjuna; I am both what is and what is not.
9.20 [Krishna] Those versed in the three Vedas, who drink the soma and are cleansed of sin, worship me through sacrifices and pray to reach heaven. Earning merit, they reach the world of Indra, king of the gods, and enjoy the heavenly pleasures of the gods.
9.21 [Krishna] Having enjoyed that vast heaven, they return to the world of mortals once their merit runs out. So those who follow the Vedic rites while craving pleasures keep coming and going.
9.22 [Krishna] But those who worship me alone, thinking of nothing else, always devoted, I myself provide for them: I bring what they lack and protect what they have.
9.23 [Krishna] Even those who devotedly worship other gods with faith are in truth worshipping me alone, son of Kunti, though not in the proper way.
9.24 [Krishna] For I alone am the one who receives every sacrifice and the Lord of them all. But they do not know me as I truly am, and so they fall back.
9.25 [Krishna] Those devoted to the gods go to the gods; those devoted to the ancestors go to the ancestors; those who worship spirits go to the spirits; and those who worship me come to me.
9.26 [Krishna] If someone lovingly brings me just a leaf, a blossom, some fruit or a little water, I gladly partake of that gift, offered in devotion by a person of disciplined heart.
9.27 [Krishna] Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer in sacrifice, whatever you give, whatever discipline you practise, son of Kunti, do it as an offering to me.
9.28 [Krishna] Living this way, you will break free of the chains of action, whose results are pleasant or painful. With your heart joined to me through renunciation and yoga, you will be released and reach me.
9.29 [Krishna] I hold every creature in equal regard; I dislike no one and favour no one. Yet those who turn to me in loving devotion live in me, and I live in them as well.
9.30 [Krishna] Even if a person of very bad conduct worships me with undivided devotion, he should be regarded as good, for he has made the right resolve.
9.31 [Krishna] He quickly becomes righteous and attains lasting peace. Son of Kunti, declare it boldly: my devotee never perishes.
9.32 [Krishna] Those who take refuge in me, son of Pritha, even those born in lowly circumstances, women, merchants and labourers, they too reach the highest goal.
9.33 [Krishna] How much more, then, the virtuous brahmins and devoted royal sages! Having come into this passing, joyless world, turn to me in devotion.
9.34 [Krishna] Fix your mind on me, be devoted to me, worship me and bow down to me. Disciplining yourself this way, with me as your highest aim, you will surely come to me.

# Chapter 10: Vibhooti Yoga (Divine Glories)
10.1 [Krishna] Krishna said: Listen once more, mighty Arjuna, to my supreme words. Because you delight in them, I will speak for your good.
10.2 [Krishna] How I came to be is unknown to the many gods and to the great seers alike, since it is from me that every god and every great seer first arose.
10.3 [Krishna] Whoever knows me as unborn and without beginning, the great Lord of the worlds, is free of delusion among mortals and is released from all sins.
10.4 [Krishna] Understanding, knowledge, clarity, forgiveness, truthfulness, self-restraint, calm, pleasure and pain, birth and death, fear and fearlessness…
10.5 [Krishna] …non-violence, evenness of mind, contentment, austerity, generosity, fame and disgrace: all these different states of beings arise from me alone.
10.6 [Krishna] The seven great sages of old and the four Manus before them came from my being, born of my mind. From them come all the creatures of this world.
10.7 [Krishna] Whoever truly understands this glory and power of mine is united with me in unwavering yoga. Of this there is no doubt.
10.8 [Krishna] I am the origin of everything; from me everything unfolds. Understanding this, the wise worship me with deep feeling.
10.9 [Krishna] With their minds on me and their lives given to me, enlightening one another and always speaking of me, they are content and full of joy.
10.10 [Krishna] To those who are always devoted and worship me with love, I give the yoga of understanding by which they come to me.
10.11 [Krishna] Because I care for them, I take my place within their being and drive away the gloom that ignorance breeds, using the bright lamp of knowledge.
10.12 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: You are the supreme Brahman, the supreme abode, the supreme purifier. You are the eternal, divine Person, the first of the gods, unborn and all-pervading.
10.13 [Arjuna] All the sages say this of you, as do the divine sage Narada, and Asita, Devala and Vyasa. And now you yourself tell me so.
10.14 [Arjuna] I accept as true all that you tell me, Krishna. Lord, neither the gods nor the demons understand your true manifestation.
10.15 [Arjuna] You alone know yourself through yourself, O Supreme Person, source and Lord of all beings, God of gods, master of the world.
10.16 [Arjuna] Please describe to me in full your divine glories, the manifestations through which you pervade these worlds and abide in them.
10.17 [Arjuna] Master of yoga, how may I know you as I constantly meditate on you? In which forms and aspects, Lord, should I think of you?
10.18 [Arjuna] Tell me again in detail, Krishna, about your power and your glories. I never tire of hearing your nectar-like words.
10.19 [Krishna] Krishna said: Very well, Arjuna, best of the Kurus, I will tell you of my divine glories, but only the chief ones, for there is no end to their full extent.
10.20 [Krishna] Gudakesha, I am the Self that lives deep within every creature. Every being begins in me, continues in me, and comes to its close in me.
10.21 [Krishna] Among the Adityas I am Vishnu; among lights, the radiant sun. Among the Maruts I am Marichi; among the stars, I am the moon.
10.22 [Krishna] Of the Vedas, the Sama is me; of the gods, Vasava (Indra). Of the senses I am the mind, and within creatures I am the power of consciousness.
10.23 [Krishna] Among the Rudras I am Shankara; among the yakshas and rakshasas, Kubera, lord of wealth. Among the Vasus I am fire, and among mountain peaks I am Meru.
10.24 [Krishna] Among priests, son of Pritha, know me to be the foremost, Brihaspati. Among generals I am Skanda, and among bodies of water I am the ocean.
10.25 [Krishna] Among the great sages I am Bhrigu; among words, the single syllable Om. Among offerings I am the quiet repetition of a sacred name, and among things that stand firm, the Himalaya.
10.26 [Krishna] Among all trees I am the sacred fig; among divine sages, Narada. Among the gandharvas I am Chitraratha, and among the perfected ones, the sage Kapila.
10.27 [Krishna] Among horses, know me as Uchchaihshravas, born of the nectar churned from the ocean. Among lordly elephants I am Airavata, and among men, the king.
10.28 [Krishna] Among weapons I am the thunderbolt; among cows, Kamadhenu, the wish-granting cow. I am Kandarpa, the god of love who brings forth life, and among serpents I am Vasuki.
10.29 [Krishna] Among the nagas I am Ananta; among water beings, Varuna. Among the ancestors I am Aryaman, and among those who keep order, I am Yama.
10.30 [Krishna] Among the Daityas I am Prahlada; among those who measure, I am time. Among animals I am the lion, and among birds, Garuda, son of Vinata.
10.31 [Krishna] Among purifiers I am the wind; among those who bear arms, Rama. Among sea creatures I am the makara, and among rivers, the Ganga.
10.32 [Krishna] Of all creations I am the beginning, the end and the middle, Arjuna. Among the branches of knowledge I am knowledge of the Self, and among those who debate, I am the reasoning that seeks the truth.
10.33 [Krishna] Of all the letters, I am 'A'; of the kinds of compound words, I am the dual. I myself am time that never runs out, and I am the ordainer who faces in all directions.
10.34 [Krishna] I am death that carries everything away, and the source of all that is yet to be. Among feminine qualities I am fame, prosperity, speech, memory, intelligence, steadfastness and forbearance.
10.35 [Krishna] Among Sama hymns I am the Brihat Saman; among metres, the Gayatri. Among months I am Margashirsha, and among seasons, flower-bearing spring.
10.36 [Krishna] Of cheats I am gambling; of the brilliant, I am their brilliance. I am victory, I am resolve, and I am the goodness of the good.
10.37 [Krishna] Among the Vrishnis I am Vasudeva; among the Pandavas, Arjuna. Among sages I am Vyasa, and among poets, the seer Ushana.
10.38 [Krishna] Of those who discipline I am the rod of punishment; of those who seek victory, wise strategy. Of secrets I am silence, and of the wise, their wisdom.
10.39 [Krishna] Whatever is the seed of all beings, Arjuna, that I am. Nothing, moving or unmoving, could exist without me.
10.40 [Krishna] There is no end to my divine glories, Arjuna. What I have told you is only a brief sketch of their vastness.
10.41 [Krishna] Whatever is glorious, beautiful or mighty, know that it has sprung from a mere spark of my splendour.
10.42 [Krishna] But what need is there, Arjuna, for you to know all this in detail? I stand supporting this entire universe with a single fraction of myself.

# Chapter 11: Vishwaroopa Darshana Yoga (The Vision of the Cosmic Form)
11.1 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: Out of kindness to me, you have spoken the supreme secret concerning the Self. Through these words my confusion is gone.
11.2 [Arjuna] Lotus-eyed Krishna, I have heard from you in detail how beings come into existence and pass away, and also of your own imperishable greatness.
11.3 [Arjuna] Supreme Lord, it is just as you have described yourself. Yet I long to see your divine, sovereign form, O Supreme Person.
11.4 [Arjuna] Lord, if you think it is possible for me to see it, then, Master of yoga, show me your imperishable Self.
11.5 [Krishna] Krishna said: Behold, son of Pritha, my forms in their hundreds and thousands, divine and of many kinds, in many colours and shapes.
11.6 [Krishna] See the Adityas, the Vasus, the Rudras, the two Ashvins and the Maruts. See many wonders never seen before, Arjuna.
11.7 [Krishna] Here, today, see the whole universe, with all that moves and does not move, gathered in one place within my body, Arjuna, along with anything else you wish to see.
11.8 [Krishna] But you cannot see me with these eyes of yours. I give you divine sight: behold my sovereign power.
11.9 [Sanjaya] Sanjaya said: O King, having spoken thus, Hari, the great Lord of yoga, then revealed to Arjuna his supreme, divine form.
11.10 [Sanjaya] It had countless mouths and eyes and countless wondrous sights, many divine ornaments, and many divine weapons held aloft.
11.11 [Sanjaya] He wore divine garlands and garments, was anointed with divine fragrances, and was full of every wonder: the boundless God, his faces turned in every direction.
11.12 [Sanjaya] If the light of a thousand suns were to blaze up in the sky all at once, it might come close to the splendour of that mighty Being.
11.13 [Sanjaya] At that moment Arjuna, the son of Pandu, beheld the whole cosmos, split into countless parts yet standing as one, inside the form of the Lord of lords.
11.14 [Sanjaya] Then Arjuna, overcome with wonder and his hair standing on end, bowed his head to the Lord and spoke with joined palms.
11.15 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: O God, in your body I see all the gods and hosts of every kind of being; Brahma the creator seated on his lotus, all the sages and the divine serpents.
11.16 [Arjuna] I see you everywhere, endless in form, with countless arms, bellies, mouths and eyes. Lord of the universe, whose form is the universe, I see no end in you, no middle and no beginning.
11.17 [Arjuna] I see you crowned, bearing a mace and a discus, a mass of radiance glowing on all sides, hard to look at, blazing everywhere like burning fire and the sun, beyond all measure.
11.18 [Arjuna] You are the imperishable, the Supreme to be known. You are the final resting place of this universe. You are the undying guardian of eternal dharma; I know you to be the timeless Person.
11.19 [Arjuna] I see you without beginning, middle or end, infinite in power, with countless arms, the sun and moon as your eyes, your mouth a blazing fire, scorching this universe with your radiance.
11.20 [Arjuna] You alone fill the space between heaven and earth and all the directions. Seeing this wondrous, terrifying form of yours, great one, the three worlds tremble.
11.21 [Arjuna] Hosts of gods are entering into you; some, in fear, praise you with joined palms. Crying 'May all be well!', bands of great sages and perfected ones sing your praise with abundant hymns.
11.22 [Arjuna] The Rudras, Adityas, Vasus and Sadhyas, the Vishvadevas, the Ashvins, the Maruts and the ancestors, and hosts of gandharvas, yakshas, asuras and perfected ones all gaze at you in amazement.
11.23 [Arjuna] Mighty one, seeing your vast form with its many mouths and eyes, many arms, thighs and feet, many bellies and many terrible fangs, the worlds are shaken, and so am I.
11.24 [Arjuna] Seeing you touching the sky, blazing in many colours, with mouths gaping wide and huge flaming eyes, my innermost self trembles, Vishnu, and I find neither courage nor peace.
11.25 [Arjuna] Seeing your mouths with their dreadful fangs, like the fire that ends the world, I lose all sense of direction and find no shelter. Have mercy, Lord of gods, refuge of the universe!
11.26 [Arjuna] All these sons of Dhritarashtra, together with hosts of kings, Bhishma, Drona and Karna, son of the charioteer, along with our own chief warriors too…
11.27 [Arjuna] …are rushing into your terrifying mouths with their dreadful fangs. Some can be seen caught between your teeth, their heads crushed to powder.
11.28 [Arjuna] As the many torrents of rivers race headlong towards the ocean, so these heroes of the human world are entering your flaming mouths.
11.29 [Arjuna] As moths rush at full speed into a blazing flame to their destruction, so these people rush at full speed into your mouths to their destruction.
11.30 [Arjuna] With blazing jaws you swallow every world around you and keep licking at them. Vishnu, your terrible flames fill all creation with their glare and set it burning.
11.31 [Arjuna] Tell me, who are you, so fierce in form? I bow to you, best of gods; have mercy! I wish to understand you, the Primal One, for I cannot grasp what you are doing.
11.32 [Krishna] Krishna said: I am Time, swollen to full strength, the one who brings worlds to ruin, and I have come here to gather these people in. Whether or not you act, every fighter lined up on the other side will perish.
11.33 [Krishna] So rise up and win glory! Conquer your enemies and enjoy a prosperous kingdom. They have already been slain by me; you, Arjuna, be merely the instrument.
11.34 [Krishna] Drona, Bhishma, Jayadratha, Karna and the other brave warriors have already been slain by me. Kill them, and do not be troubled. Fight! You will defeat your rivals in battle.
11.35 [Sanjaya] Sanjaya said: Hearing these words of Krishna, Arjuna, the crowned one, trembling and with joined palms, bowed down. Overcome with fear, he bowed again and spoke to Krishna in a choking voice.
11.36 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: It is fitting, Krishna, that the world rejoices and delights in praising you. Terrified demons flee in all directions, and the hosts of perfected ones all bow to you.
11.37 [Arjuna] And why should they not bow to you, great one, who are greater even than Brahma, the first creator? Infinite One, Lord of gods, home of the universe, you are the imperishable: what is, what is not, and what lies beyond both.
11.38 [Arjuna] You are the first of the gods, the ancient Person, the final resting place of this universe. You are the knower and what is to be known, and the highest abode. The universe is pervaded by you, O you of endless forms.
11.39 [Arjuna] You are Vayu, Yama, Agni, Varuna and the moon. You are Prajapati, the father of creatures, and the great-grandsire of all. Salutations to you a thousand times, and again and again, salutations to you!
11.40 [Arjuna] Salutations to you in front and behind; salutations to you on every side, you who are all. Your strength is infinite and your might immeasurable. You pervade everything, and so you are everything.
11.41 [Arjuna] Thinking of you as a friend, I carelessly called out 'O Krishna! O Yadava! O friend!', not knowing this greatness of yours, whether out of negligence or out of affection…
11.42 [Arjuna] …and if I ever showed you disrespect in jest, while playing, resting, sitting or eating, whether alone or in front of others, Krishna, I beg forgiveness from you, the immeasurable one.
11.43 [Arjuna] You are father to this whole world, to all things moving and still. It honours you as its most revered guru. In all three worlds nobody matches you, Lord of boundless might; how could anyone exceed you?
11.44 [Arjuna] So, bowing and laying my body down before you, I seek your grace, adorable Lord. As a father bears with his son, a friend with a friend, a lover with the beloved, so please bear with me, O God.
11.45 [Arjuna] I am thrilled to have seen what was never seen before, yet my mind is shaken with fear. Show me, O God, that other form of yours. Have mercy, Lord of gods, refuge of the universe.
11.46 [Arjuna] I wish to see you as before, wearing your crown and holding the mace and discus. Take on that four-armed form, O thousand-armed one, whose body is the universe.
11.47 [Krishna] Krishna said: Out of my grace, Arjuna, through my own power I have shown you this supreme form, radiant, universal, infinite and primal, which no one but you has ever seen.
11.48 [Krishna] Neither Vedic learning, ritual sacrifice nor study, neither gifts nor ceremonies nor fierce penance can reveal me in this shape to anyone in the world of mortals except you, foremost of the Kuru warriors.
11.49 [Krishna] Do not be afraid or bewildered at seeing this terrible form of mine. Free from fear and with a glad heart, look once again upon my familiar form.
11.50 [Sanjaya] Sanjaya said: Having spoken thus to Arjuna, Krishna showed his own form once more. Taking on a gentle form again, the great one comforted the frightened Arjuna.
11.51 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: Krishna, seeing this gentle human form of yours, I am now composed, and my mind has returned to its natural state.
11.52 [Krishna] Krishna said: This form of mine that you have seen is very hard to see. Even the gods always long for a sight of it.
11.53 [Krishna] Not through the Vedas, nor through austerity, charity or sacrifice can I be seen as you have seen me.
11.54 [Krishna] But through devotion directed to me alone, Arjuna, I can be known in this way, seen as I truly am, and even entered into.
11.55 [Krishna] Whoever works for me, holds me as the highest, is devoted to me, free from attachment and without hatred towards any being, comes to me, Arjuna.

# Chapter 12: Bhakti Yoga (The Path of Devotion)
12.1 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: Some devotees, always steadfast, worship you in this way; others worship the imperishable, the unmanifest. Of these, who knows yoga best?
12.2 [Krishna] Krishna said: Those who fix their mind on me and worship me, ever united with me and filled with supreme faith, I regard as the most perfect in yoga.
12.3 [Krishna] But those who worship the imperishable, which cannot be defined, which is unmanifest, present everywhere, beyond thought, unchanging at the core, unmoving and constant…
12.4 [Krishna] …holding all their senses in check, even-minded toward everything and delighting in the welfare of all beings, they too reach me alone.
12.5 [Krishna] Their struggle is far greater, since their minds are set on the unmanifest; for embodied beings, the path to the unmanifest is reached only with hardship.
12.6 [Krishna] But those who surrender all their actions to me, hold me as their highest goal, and worship me by meditating on me with undivided yoga…
12.7 [Krishna] …for them, whose minds have entered into me, I soon become the one who lifts them out of the ocean of death and repeated birth, Arjuna.
12.8 [Krishna] Fix your mind on me alone and let your understanding settle in me. From then on you will live in me; of this there is no doubt.
12.9 [Krishna] If you cannot hold your mind steadily on me, then seek to reach me through the yoga of repeated practice, Arjuna.
12.10 [Krishna] If you are unable even to practise, make work done for me your highest aim. Even by acting for my sake, you will reach perfection.
12.11 [Krishna] And if you cannot do even this, then take refuge in my yoga, master yourself, and let go of the fruits of all your actions.
12.12 [Krishna] Knowledge is better than practice; meditation is greater than knowledge; letting go of the fruits of action is greater than meditation, and peace follows that letting go at once.
12.13 [Krishna] One who bears ill will toward no being, who is friendly and compassionate, free of possessiveness and ego, even-minded in pain and pleasure, and forgiving…
12.14 [Krishna] …ever content, a yogi, self-controlled, firm in resolve, with mind and understanding offered to me: such a devotee of mine is dear to me.
12.15 [Krishna] One who does not unsettle the world and is not unsettled by it, who is free from elation, resentment, fear and agitation, is also dear to me.
12.16 [Krishna] One who expects nothing, who is pure, capable, impartial and untroubled, and who has given up all self-driven undertakings: such a devotee of mine is dear to me.
12.17 [Krishna] One who neither rejoices nor hates, neither grieves nor craves, and has let go of both good and bad: that devoted person is dear to me.
12.18 [Krishna] One who is the same toward enemy and friend, in honour and dishonour, in cold and heat, in pleasure and pain, and who is free from attachment…
12.19 [Krishna] …who takes blame and praise alike, speaks little, is content with whatever comes, is tied to no home and is steady of mind: that devoted person is dear to me.
12.20 [Krishna] But those who follow this immortal teaching of dharma exactly as I have given it, full of faith and holding me as their highest goal, are exceedingly dear to me.

# Chapter 13: Ksetra Ksetrajna Vibhaaga Yoga (The Field and Its Knower)
13.1 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: Keshava, I want to learn about Prakriti and Purusha, about the field and the one who knows the field, and about knowledge and that which knowledge aims at.
13.2 [Krishna] Krishna said: This body, son of Kunti, is called the field. Those who understand these things call the one who knows it the knower of the field.
13.3 [Krishna] Know me also to be the knower of the field in every field, Arjuna. Knowledge of the field and of its knower: that, in my view, is true knowledge.
13.4 [Krishna] Hear from me briefly what the field is, what it is like, what changes it goes through and where it comes from; and also who its knower is and what powers he has.
13.5 [Krishna] The sages have sung of this in many ways, separately in various Vedic hymns, and also in the well-reasoned and conclusive verses of the Brahma Sutras.
13.6 [Krishna] The great elements, the ego-sense, the intellect and the unmanifest; the ten senses and the one mind; and the five objects of the senses…
13.7 [Krishna] …desire, aversion, pleasure, pain, the bodily aggregate, awareness and steadiness: this, in brief, is the field together with its changes.
13.8 [Krishna] Humility, freedom from pretence, non-violence, patience, honesty, service to one's teacher, purity, steadiness and self-control…
13.9 [Krishna] …dispassion toward the objects of the senses, absence of ego, and clearly seeing the suffering and flaws in birth, death, old age and disease…
13.10 [Krishna] …non-attachment, not being entangled in children, spouse, home and the like, and a constant evenness of mind whether the wanted or the unwanted comes…
13.11 [Krishna] …unwavering devotion to me through undivided yoga, a liking for solitary places and no taste for crowds…
13.12 [Krishna] …constancy in knowledge of the Self, and keeping in view the purpose of knowing the truth. This is called knowledge; whatever is contrary to it is ignorance.
13.13 [Krishna] I will now tell you what is to be known, knowing which one attains immortality: the beginningless supreme Brahman, which is said to be neither being nor non-being.
13.14 [Krishna] With hands and feet everywhere, with eyes, heads and faces everywhere, with ears everywhere, it abides in the world, enveloping all.
13.15 [Krishna] It shines through the workings of all the senses, yet is free of every sense; unattached, yet it upholds all; beyond the gunas, yet it experiences the gunas.
13.16 [Krishna] It is outside and inside all beings, unmoving and yet moving. Being so subtle, it cannot be grasped; it is far away, and yet it is near.
13.17 [Krishna] Undivided, it yet seems to stand divided among beings. Know it as the sustainer of beings, the one that devours them and the one that brings them forth.
13.18 [Krishna] It is the light of all lights and is said to be beyond darkness. It is knowledge, the thing to be known and the goal of knowledge, seated in the heart of everyone.
13.19 [Krishna] Thus the field, knowledge and the knowable have been briefly described. My devotee, understanding this, becomes fit to enter my state of being.
13.20 [Krishna] Know that prakriti and purusha are both without beginning, and know that all changes and the gunas arise from prakriti.
13.21 [Krishna] Prakriti is said to be the cause in producing body and senses and in all doing; purusha is said to be the cause in the experiencing of pleasure and pain.
13.22 [Krishna] Purusha, dwelling in prakriti, experiences the gunas born of prakriti. Its attachment to the gunas is the cause of its births in good and bad wombs.
13.23 [Krishna] The supreme purusha in this body is also called the witness, the one who permits, the sustainer, the experiencer, the great Lord and the supreme Self.
13.24 [Krishna] Whoever knows purusha, and prakriti together with its gunas, in this way is not born again, however they may be living.
13.25 [Krishna] Some see the Self in themselves, through themselves, by meditation; others through the yoga of knowledge; and still others through the yoga of action.
13.26 [Krishna] Yet others, not knowing this, worship after hearing it from others. They too, devoted to what they have heard, cross beyond death.
13.27 [Krishna] Whatever comes into being, moving or unmoving, know, Arjuna, that it arises from the union of the field and the knower of the field.
13.28 [Krishna] One who sees the supreme Lord dwelling equally in all beings, the undying within the dying, truly sees.
13.29 [Krishna] Seeing the same Lord present equally everywhere, one does not harm the Self by the self, and so reaches the highest goal.
13.30 [Krishna] One who sees that all actions are done in every way by prakriti alone, and that the Self is not the doer, truly sees.
13.31 [Krishna] When one sees the separate existence of beings as resting in the One, and their spreading out from that One alone, one attains Brahman.
13.32 [Krishna] Being without beginning and beyond the gunas, this supreme Self is imperishable. Though it dwells in the body, son of Kunti, it neither acts nor is tainted.
13.33 [Krishna] Just as all-pervading space, being subtle, is not stained by anything, so the Self, present throughout the body, is not stained.
13.34 [Krishna] As the one sun lights up this whole world, so the owner of the field lights up the whole field, Arjuna.
13.35 [Krishna] Those who see with the eye of knowledge this difference between the field and its knower, and how beings are freed from prakriti, reach the Supreme.

# Chapter 14: Gunatraya Vibhaga Yoga (The Three Gunas)
14.1 [Krishna] Krishna said: I will speak again of the highest knowledge, the finest of all knowledge, by knowing which all the sages passed from this world to the supreme perfection.
14.2 [Krishna] Relying on this knowledge, people grow into a nature like mine. When a new creation begins, they are not born again, and when everything dissolves, they feel no distress.
14.3 [Krishna] The great Brahman is my womb; in it I place the seed. From that, Arjuna, all beings come into existence.
14.4 [Krishna] Whatever forms are born in any womb, son of Kunti, the great Brahman is their womb, and I am the father who gives the seed.
14.5 [Krishna] Sattva, rajas and tamas: these gunas, born of prakriti, bind the imperishable embodied self to the body, mighty Arjuna.
14.6 [Krishna] Among the three, sattva is spotless, so it brings light and health. Even so, sinless Arjuna, it ties a person down by making them cling to happiness and to knowing.
14.7 [Krishna] Know rajas to be passion by nature, springing from craving and attachment. It binds the embodied self, son of Kunti, through attachment to action.
14.8 [Krishna] Understand that tamas arises from ignorance and clouds the minds of all embodied beings. Bharata, it holds them captive through heedlessness, sloth and sleep.
14.9 [Krishna] Sattva ties one to happiness and rajas to action, Arjuna; but tamas, covering knowledge, ties one to carelessness.
14.10 [Krishna] Sattva rises by overpowering rajas and tamas, Arjuna; rajas, by overpowering sattva and tamas; and tamas, likewise, by overpowering sattva and rajas.
14.11 [Krishna] When the light of knowledge shines through all the gates of this body, then know that sattva has grown strong.
14.12 [Krishna] Greed, busy activity, the starting of ventures, restlessness and longing: these arise when rajas grows strong, Arjuna.
14.13 [Krishna] Darkness, inertia, carelessness and delusion: these arise when tamas grows strong, Arjuna.
14.14 [Krishna] If an embodied being meets death when sattva is strong, it reaches the pure worlds of those who know the highest.
14.15 [Krishna] Dying when rajas prevails, one is born among those attached to action; dying in tamas, one is born in the wombs of the dull-witted.
14.16 [Krishna] Good deeds, the sages say, yield a sattvic and spotless result. What rajas produces is suffering, and what tamas produces is ignorance.
14.17 [Krishna] From sattva comes knowledge, from rajas greed; from tamas come carelessness and delusion, and ignorance as well.
14.18 [Krishna] Those rooted in sattva rise upward; the rajasic stay in the middle; and the tamasic, caught in the lowest workings of the gunas, go downward.
14.19 [Krishna] When the seer sees no doer other than the gunas, and knows what lies beyond the gunas, that person attains my state.
14.20 [Krishna] Going beyond these three gunas, from which the body arises, the embodied one is freed from birth, death, old age and sorrow, and tastes immortality.
14.21 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: Master, what marks a person who has passed beyond these three gunas? How does that person behave, and by what means do they move past the three gunas?
14.22 [Krishna] Krishna said: Such a person does not resent light, activity or delusion when they arise, son of Pandu, nor long for them when they fade away.
14.23 [Krishna] One who sits as if unconcerned, unshaken by the gunas, who stays firm and does not waver, knowing simply that it is the gunas that are at work…
14.24 [Krishna] …who is the same in pain and pleasure, at rest in the Self, who sees a clod of earth, a stone and gold alike, who is steady, and the same toward the pleasant and the unpleasant, toward blame and praise of himself…
14.25 [Krishna] …the same in honour and dishonour, the same toward friend and foe, who has given up all self-driven undertakings: such a person is said to have gone beyond the gunas.
14.26 [Krishna] One who serves me with unwavering bhakti yoga rises above these gunas and becomes fit to become one with Brahman.
14.27 [Krishna] For I am the foundation of Brahman, of the immortal and imperishable, of eternal dharma and of complete and lasting happiness.

# Chapter 15: Purushottama Yoga (The Highest Person)
15.1 [Krishna] Krishna said: They speak of an imperishable ashvattha tree with its roots above and its branches below. Its leaves are the Vedic hymns; whoever knows this tree knows the Vedas.
15.2 [Krishna] Its branches spread below and above, fed by the gunas, with sense objects as their buds. Below, in the world of humans, its roots stretch out, binding through action.
15.3 [Krishna] Its real form cannot be seen here, nor its end, its beginning or its base. Cutting down this firmly rooted ashvattha with the strong axe of detachment…
15.4 [Krishna] …one should then seek that place from which those who reach it never return, thinking: I take refuge in that primal purusha from whom this ancient flow of existence came forth.
15.5 [Krishna] Free from pride and delusion, having overcome the flaw of attachment, ever dwelling on the Self, with desires turned away and released from the pairs called pleasure and pain, the undeluded reach that eternal state.
15.6 [Krishna] Neither the sun, nor the moon, nor fire illumines it. Those who go there do not return; that is my supreme abode.
15.7 [Krishna] An eternal fragment of my own self, becoming a living soul in the world of living beings, draws to itself the senses, with the mind as the sixth, which rest in prakriti.
15.8 [Krishna] When the lord of the body takes on a body and when it leaves one, it carries these along with it, as the wind carries scents from their source.
15.9 [Krishna] Taking its station in hearing, sight and touch, in taste and smell, and in the mind as well, this embodied self experiences the world of the senses.
15.10 [Krishna] The deluded do not see it, whether it is departing or staying, or enjoying while joined with the gunas; those with the eye of knowledge do see it.
15.11 [Krishna] Yogis who strive see it established within themselves; but those whose minds are unrefined and unaware do not see it, even when they strive.
15.12 [Krishna] The radiance in the sun that lights up the whole world, and the radiance in the moon and in fire: know that radiance to be mine.
15.13 [Krishna] Entering the earth, I sustain all beings with my vital power; and becoming Soma, the essence of sap, I nourish all plants.
15.14 [Krishna] Becoming the fire of digestion, I dwell in the bodies of living beings, and joined with the incoming and outgoing breath, I digest the four kinds of food.
15.15 [Krishna] I dwell within every heart. Remembering, knowing and forgetting all arise from me. Every Veda points to me as what must be known; I composed Vedanta, and I am the one who understands the Vedas.
15.16 [Krishna] Two kinds of being exist in this world: one that perishes and one that does not. Every creature belongs to the perishing kind, while the changeless one, firm as an anvil, is named the undecaying.
15.17 [Krishna] But there is another, the highest purusha, called the supreme Self: the imperishable Lord who enters the three worlds and upholds them.
15.18 [Krishna] Because I am beyond the perishable and higher even than the imperishable, I am known in the world and in the Veda as Purushottama, the highest purusha.
15.19 [Krishna] Whoever, free from delusion, knows me in this way as the highest purusha knows everything, and worships me with their whole being, Arjuna.
15.20 [Krishna] Thus I have taught you this most secret teaching, sinless Arjuna. Understanding it, one becomes truly wise and has done all that needs to be done.

# Chapter 16: Daivasura Sampad Vibhaga Yoga (Divine and Demonic Natures)
16.1 [Krishna] Krishna said: Fearlessness, purity of heart, steadiness in the yoga of knowledge, generosity, self-restraint, sacrifice, study of scripture, austerity, straightforwardness…
16.2 [Krishna] …non-violence, truthfulness, freedom from anger, renunciation, calm, not speaking ill of others, compassion for all beings, freedom from greed, gentleness, modesty and lack of fickleness…
16.3 [Krishna] …vigour, forgiveness, fortitude, cleanliness, freedom from malice and from excessive pride: these belong to one born with the divine nature, Arjuna.
16.4 [Krishna] Hypocrisy, arrogance, conceit, anger, harshness and ignorance belong to one born with the demonic nature, Arjuna.
16.5 [Krishna] The divine nature leads to liberation, and the demonic to bondage. Do not worry, Arjuna; you are born with the divine nature.
16.6 [Krishna] There are two kinds of beings created in this world, the divine and the demonic. The divine has been described at length; now hear from me about the demonic, Arjuna.
16.7 [Krishna] People of demonic nature do not know when to act and when to hold back. Neither purity, nor good conduct, nor truth is found in them.
16.8 [Krishna] They say the world is unreal, without foundation and without God; that it comes about from mere union of one thing with another, caused by desire and nothing else.
16.9 [Krishna] Holding fast to this view, these lost souls of little understanding, given to cruel deeds, rise up as enemies of the world, bent on its destruction.
16.10 [Krishna] Clinging to desire that can never be filled, full of hypocrisy, pride and arrogance, seizing on false ideas out of delusion, they act with impure resolves.
16.11 [Krishna] Absorbed in endless worries that last until death, treating the enjoyment of desires as the highest aim, they are convinced that this is all there is.
16.12 [Krishna] Bound by hundreds of ropes of hope, given over to desire and anger, they strive to pile up wealth by unjust means to feed their cravings.
16.13 [Krishna] “This I have gained today; this wish I will fulfil next. This wealth is mine, and more will be mine too.”
16.14 [Krishna] “I have killed that enemy, and I will kill the others too. I am the master, I am the enjoyer; I am successful, powerful and happy.”
16.15 [Krishna] “I am wealthy and of high birth; who else is like me? I will offer sacrifices, I will give charity, I will celebrate.” So they think, deluded by ignorance.
16.16 [Krishna] Confused by many thoughts, caught in a net of delusion and addicted to the enjoyment of desires, they fall into a foul hell.
16.17 [Krishna] Self-important and stubborn, intoxicated by wealth and status, they perform sacrifices in name only, with show and without the proper rites.
16.18 [Krishna] Leaning on egotism, power, pride, lust and rage, such malicious people despise me, though I live within their bodies as I do within everyone else's.
16.19 [Krishna] These hateful and cruel people, the lowest of humans and full of evil, I cast again and again into demonic wombs within the cycle of births.
16.20 [Krishna] Falling into demonic wombs, deluded birth after birth, they never reach me, son of Kunti, and sink to a still lower state.
16.21 [Krishna] Desire, anger and greed: these are the three gates of hell that bring ruin to the self. Therefore one should give up all three.
16.22 [Krishna] A person freed from these three gates of darkness, son of Kunti, does what is truly good for the self, and so reaches the highest goal.
16.23 [Krishna] One who sets aside the guidance of scripture and acts on the impulse of desire gains neither perfection, nor happiness, nor the highest goal.
16.24 [Krishna] Therefore let scripture be your guide in deciding what should and should not be done. Knowing what the scripture lays down, you should do your work here.

# Chapter 17: Sraddhatraya Vibhaga Yoga (The Three Kinds of Faith)
17.1 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: Krishna, what about those who set aside the rules of scripture yet worship with faith? Where do they stand: in sattva, rajas or tamas?
17.2 [Krishna] Krishna said: The faith of embodied beings is of three kinds, arising from their own nature: sattvic, rajasic and tamasic. Hear about it.
17.3 [Krishna] Everyone's faith takes the shape of their inner being, Arjuna. A person is made of faith: whatever one's faith is, that is what one is.
17.4 [Krishna] Sattvic people worship the gods; rajasic people worship yakshas and rakshasas; and tamasic people worship ghosts and hosts of spirits.
17.5 [Krishna] Those who practise harsh austerities that scripture does not prescribe, full of hypocrisy and ego, driven by the force of desire and attachment…
17.6 [Krishna] …who in their folly torment the elements that make up the body, and Me as well, who dwell within the body: know them to be of demonic resolve.
17.7 [Krishna] Even the food each person likes is of three kinds, and so are sacrifice, austerity and giving. Hear how they differ.
17.8 [Krishna] Foods that increase life, vitality, strength, health, happiness and contentment, and that are flavourful, rich, nourishing and pleasing to the heart, are dear to the sattvic.
17.9 [Krishna] Foods that are bitter, sour, salty, too hot, sharp, dry and burning are liked by the rajasic; they bring pain, grief and illness.
17.10 [Krishna] Food that is stale, tasteless, foul-smelling and kept overnight, food left over by others and impure, is dear to the tamasic.
17.11 [Krishna] A sacrifice offered as scripture directs, by those who want no reward, with the mind settled on the thought “this simply ought to be done”, is sattvic.
17.12 [Krishna] But a sacrifice performed with an eye on its reward, or for the sake of show, know that to be rajasic, best of the Bharatas.
17.13 [Krishna] A sacrifice done without proper procedure, without sharing food, without mantras, without gifts to the priests and without faith is called tamasic.
17.14 [Krishna] Honouring the gods, the twice-born, teachers and the wise; cleanliness, straightforwardness, celibacy and non-violence: this is called austerity of the body.
17.15 [Krishna] Words that cause no distress, that are true, kind and helpful, along with the regular practice of sacred study: this is called austerity of speech.
17.16 [Krishna] Serenity of mind, gentleness, silence, self-control and purity of heart: this is called austerity of the mind.
17.17 [Krishna] When this threefold austerity is practised with the highest faith by disciplined people who seek no reward, it is called sattvic.
17.18 [Krishna] Austerity done with hypocrisy, to gain respect, honour and reverence, is called rajasic here; it is unsteady and does not last.
17.19 [Krishna] Austerity done out of foolish obstinacy, by tormenting oneself, or in order to ruin another, is declared tamasic.
17.20 [Krishna] A gift given simply because giving is right, to one who cannot repay it, at the proper place and time and to a worthy person, is considered sattvic.
17.21 [Krishna] But a gift given to get a favour in return, or with some reward in view, or given reluctantly, is considered rajasic.
17.22 [Krishna] A gift given at the wrong place and time, to unworthy people, without respect and with contempt, is declared tamasic.
17.23 [Krishna] “Om Tat Sat”: this is known as the threefold name of Brahman. Through it, in the beginning, the brahmins, the Vedas and the sacrifices were established.
17.24 [Krishna] That is why those devoted to Brahman always begin their acts of sacrifice, giving and austerity, as laid down in scripture, by uttering “Om”.
17.25 [Krishna] With the word “Tat”, and without aiming at any reward, those who seek liberation perform their various acts of sacrifice, austerity and giving.
17.26 [Krishna] The word “Sat” is used to mean what is real and what is good; and, son of Pritha, “Sat” is also used for a praiseworthy deed.
17.27 [Krishna] Steadfastness in sacrifice, austerity and giving is also called “Sat”, and any action done for that purpose is likewise called “Sat”.
17.28 [Krishna] Whatever is offered, given, practised as austerity or done without faith is called “asat”, son of Pritha. It is of no value either in the next world or in this one.

# Chapter 18: Moksha Sanyaas Yoga (Freedom through Letting Go)
18.1 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: Mighty Krishna, I wish to understand the true nature of renunciation (sannyasa), and of relinquishment (tyaga), each distinctly.
18.2 [Krishna] Krishna said: The wise understand renunciation (sannyasa) as giving up actions driven by desire. The discerning say relinquishment (tyaga) means letting go of the fruits of all actions.
18.3 [Krishna] Some thinkers say that action should be given up because it is flawed; others say that acts of sacrifice, giving and austerity must not be given up.
18.4 [Krishna] Now hear my conclusion about relinquishment, best of the Bharatas. Relinquishment, tiger among men, is said to be of three kinds.
18.5 [Krishna] Acts of sacrifice, giving and austerity should not be abandoned; they must be done. Sacrifice, giving and austerity purify even the wise.
18.6 [Krishna] But even these acts should be done without attachment and without wanting their results. This, son of Pritha, is my firm and highest view.
18.7 [Krishna] It is not right to renounce a duty that is one's to do. Abandoning it out of delusion is called tamasic relinquishment.
18.8 [Krishna] One who drops an action because it is painful, out of fear of bodily strain, makes a rajasic relinquishment and does not gain the fruit of relinquishment.
18.9 [Krishna] When a prescribed action is done simply because it ought to be done, Arjuna, letting go of attachment and of the fruit, that relinquishment is considered sattvic.
18.10 [Krishna] The true relinquisher, filled with sattva, wise and free of doubt, neither resents unpleasant work nor clings to pleasant work.
18.11 [Krishna] No one who has a body can give up action completely. So the one who gives up the fruit of action is the one truly called a relinquisher.
18.12 [Krishna] The fruit of action is of three kinds: unwanted, wanted and mixed. It comes after death to those who have not relinquished, but never to those who have renounced.
18.13 [Krishna] Learn from me, mighty Arjuna, the five factors named in the Sankhya teaching as needed for the accomplishment of every action.
18.14 [Krishna] They are: the seat of action (the body), the doer, the various instruments, the many distinct kinds of effort, and fifth, the unseen divine factor (daiva).
18.15 [Krishna] Whatever action a person begins with body, speech or mind, whether right or wrong, these five are its causes.
18.16 [Krishna] This being so, whoever, through an untrained understanding, sees the Self alone as the doer, does not really see; his judgement is clouded.
18.17 [Krishna] Someone with no sense of being the doer, whose judgement stays unstained, is not a killer even when striking down all these people, and such deeds do not bind him.
18.18 [Krishna] Knowledge, the thing known and the knower are the three that prompt action. The instrument, the act and the doer are the three that make up action.
18.19 [Krishna] In the teaching on the gunas, knowledge, action and the doer are each said to be of three kinds, according to the gunas. Hear about these too, as they really are.
18.20 [Krishna] The knowledge by which one sees a single, imperishable reality in all beings, undivided within the divided: know that knowledge to be sattvic.
18.21 [Krishna] But the knowledge that sees in all beings many separate entities of different kinds, each apart from the rest: know that knowledge to be rajasic.
18.22 [Krishna] And the knowledge that clings to one single thing as if it were everything, without reason, without grasp of the truth, and narrow: that is called tamasic.
18.23 [Krishna] Action that is prescribed, done without attachment, without liking or dislike, by one who does not seek its fruit, is called sattvic.
18.24 [Krishna] But action done by one chasing desires, or with a sense of ego, and with great strain, is called rajasic.
18.25 [Krishna] Action begun out of delusion, without regard for its consequences, the loss it brings, the harm it does, or one's own capacity, is called tamasic.
18.26 [Krishna] A doer who is free from attachment, does not boast, is full of resolve and enthusiasm, and stays unchanged in success and failure, is called sattvic.
18.27 [Krishna] A doer who is full of passion, eager for the fruits of action, greedy, harmful by nature, impure, and tossed between elation and grief, is called rajasic.
18.28 [Krishna] A doer who is undisciplined, crude, obstinate, deceitful, malicious, lazy, gloomy and given to putting things off is called tamasic.
18.29 [Krishna] Now hear, Arjuna, the threefold division of understanding (buddhi) and of resolve (dhriti) according to the gunas, explained fully and one by one.
18.30 [Krishna] The understanding that knows when to act and when to hold back, what should and should not be done, what to fear and what not to fear, and what binds and what frees: that, son of Pritha, is sattvic.
18.31 [Krishna] The understanding by which one grasps dharma and adharma, and what should and should not be done, only in a distorted way: that, son of Pritha, is rajasic.
18.32 [Krishna] The understanding that, covered in darkness, takes adharma for dharma and sees everything the wrong way round: that, son of Pritha, is tamasic.
18.33 [Krishna] The unwavering resolve by which, through yoga, one holds steady the workings of the mind, the life-breath and the senses: that, son of Pritha, is sattvic.
18.34 [Krishna] But the resolve by which one holds on to duty, pleasure and wealth, Arjuna, out of attachment and craving their fruits: that, son of Pritha, is rajasic.
18.35 [Krishna] The resolve by which a dull-minded person will not let go of sleep, fear, grief, despair and arrogance: that, son of Pritha, is tamasic.
18.36 [Krishna] Now hear from me, best of the Bharatas, about the three kinds of happiness. The happiness one comes to enjoy through practice, and in which sorrow comes to an end…
18.37 [Krishna] …which is like poison at first but like nectar in the end, and which springs from the clarity of one's own mind: that happiness is called sattvic.
18.38 [Krishna] Happiness that comes from the senses meeting their objects, like nectar at first but like poison in the end, is considered rajasic.
18.39 [Krishna] Happiness that deludes the self both at the start and in what follows, arising from sleep, sloth and carelessness, is declared tamasic.
18.40 [Krishna] Nowhere on earth, and nowhere among the gods in heaven, does any being exist that is untouched by these three gunas, which spring from Prakriti.
18.41 [Krishna] The duties of brahmins, kshatriyas and vaishyas, and of shudras, Arjuna, are divided according to the qualities that arise from their own nature.
18.42 [Krishna] Calmness, self-restraint, austerity, purity, forbearance, uprightness, knowledge, realised wisdom and faith in the sacred: these make up a brahmin's work, born of his nature.
18.43 [Krishna] Courage, vigour, steadiness, skill, not fleeing from battle, generosity and the bearing of a leader: these make up a kshatriya's work, born of his nature.
18.44 [Krishna] Farming, tending cattle and trade are a vaishya's work, born of his nature; and work that takes the form of service is a shudra's, also born of his nature.
18.45 [Krishna] Devoted each to their own work, people attain fulfilment. Hear how one who is dedicated to their own work reaches that perfection.
18.46 [Krishna] By worshipping through one's own work the One from whom all beings come forth and by whom all this is pervaded, a person attains perfection.
18.47 [Krishna] Better one's own dharma, though imperfectly done, than another's dharma done well. Doing the work set by one's own nature, one incurs no wrong.
18.48 [Krishna] Do not abandon the work that is natural to you, son of Kunti, even if it has flaws. Every undertaking is clouded by some flaw, as fire is by smoke.
18.49 [Krishna] One whose mind is unattached everywhere, who has mastered himself and is free of craving, attains through renunciation the supreme perfection of freedom from action.
18.50 [Krishna] Learn from me in brief, son of Kunti, how one who has reached perfection attains Brahman, the highest culmination of knowledge.
18.51 [Krishna] Equipped with a purified understanding, restraining himself with firm resolve, turning away from sound and the other objects of the senses, and casting off attraction and aversion…
18.52 [Krishna] …living in solitude, eating lightly, keeping speech, body and mind in check, always intent on the yoga of meditation, and resting in dispassion…
18.53 [Krishna] …letting go of ego, force, arrogance, desire, anger and possessiveness, free of the sense of “mine” and at peace, he becomes fit to become one with Brahman.
18.54 [Krishna] Having become one with Brahman, serene within, he neither grieves nor craves. Seeing all beings as equal, he gains supreme devotion to Me.
18.55 [Krishna] Through devotion he comes to know Me truly: how vast I am and who I am. Then, knowing Me as I truly am, he at once enters into Me.
18.56 [Krishna] Even while always doing all kinds of actions, one who takes refuge in Me reaches, by My grace, the eternal, imperishable state.
18.57 [Krishna] Inwardly offering all your actions to Me, holding Me as your highest goal and relying on the yoga of understanding, keep your mind always fixed on Me.
18.58 [Krishna] With your mind on Me, you will overcome every difficulty by My grace. But if out of ego you will not listen, you will be lost.
18.59 [Krishna] If, relying on your ego, you think “I will not fight”, that resolve of yours is empty; your own nature will drive you to it.
18.60 [Krishna] Bound by your own work, which comes from your nature, son of Kunti, you will do even against your will what in your delusion you do not wish to do.
18.61 [Krishna] The Lord dwells in the heart of all beings, Arjuna, and by His power of maya keeps them all turning, as though they were mounted on a machine.
18.62 [Krishna] Take refuge in Him alone with your whole being, Arjuna. By His grace you will reach supreme peace and the eternal abode.
18.63 [Krishna] So I have told you this knowledge, more secret than any secret. Reflect on it fully, and then do as you choose.
18.64 [Krishna] Hear once more my supreme word, the most secret of all. You are deeply dear to Me, so I will tell you what is for your good.
18.65 [Krishna] Fix your mind on Me, be devoted to Me, worship Me, bow to Me. You will surely come to Me; I truly promise you this, for you are dear to Me.
18.66 [Krishna] Let go of all dharmas and come to Me alone for refuge. I will free you from all sins; do not grieve.
18.67 [Krishna] Never speak of this to one who lacks self-discipline, who has no devotion, who does not want to listen, or who finds fault with Me.
18.68 [Krishna] Whoever shares this supreme secret with My devotees, offering the highest devotion to Me, will without doubt come to Me.
18.69 [Krishna] No one among people does Me a service dearer than that person does, and no one on earth will be dearer to Me.
18.70 [Krishna] And whoever studies this sacred dialogue of ours worships Me through the sacrifice of knowledge. That is my conviction.
18.71 [Krishna] Even one who just listens to it with faith and without finding fault will be set free, and will reach the auspicious worlds of those whose deeds are pure.
18.72 [Krishna] Have you listened to this with a focused mind, son of Pritha? Has your confusion, born of ignorance, been cleared away, Arjuna?
18.73 [Arjuna] Arjuna said: My delusion is gone, Krishna, and by your grace I have regained my clarity. I stand firm, free of doubt. I will do as you say.
18.74 [Sanjaya] Sanjaya said: Thus I heard this wondrous dialogue between Krishna, son of Vasudeva, and the great-souled son of Pritha, and it made my hair stand on end.
18.75 [Sanjaya] By the grace of Vyasa I heard this supreme and secret yoga directly from Krishna, the master of yoga, as he himself was speaking it.
18.76 [Sanjaya] O King, as I recall again and again this wondrous and holy dialogue between Krishna and Arjuna, I rejoice over and over.
18.77 [Sanjaya] And as I recall again and again that most wondrous form of Hari, my amazement is great, O King, and I rejoice again and again.
18.78 [Sanjaya] Wherever there is Krishna, the master of yoga, and wherever there is Arjuna, the archer, there will be fortune, victory, prosperity and steadfast righteousness. That is my conviction.
